## Define the problem and intent

- Rewrite the brief as: user goal, present obstacle, and harmed business metric. Do not accept an artifact request as the problem. Fails when: discovery is already complete and the assignment is explicitly execution-only. ([brief-to-outcome](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=141s))
- Express each user intent as a verb phrase before selecting screens or controls; include only inputs that materially affect the outcome. Fails when: the intent is unknown or disputed—research or state the hypothesis first; still collect legally or operationally required data. ([intent-first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=26s))
- Identify every materially different intent at an entry point and allocate an affordance to each, such as search for decided users and filtered browsing for exploratory users. Fails when: the flow has already narrowed to one intent and alternatives would distract. ([intent-first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=26s))
- Make the dominant intent and its next action unmistakable; verify that the information needed to act is present before considering visual polish. Fails when: the surface is primarily editorial, expressive, or brand-building rather than action-oriented. ([brief-to-outcome](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=141s)) ([intent-first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=26s))
- Admit a new feature or interface element only when you can name the new capability, intent, compliance obligation, or trust function it serves. Fails when: a legitimate perceptual goal such as legibility, orientation, or brand positioning is being assessed as though it were functional. ([intent-first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=26s))
- Diagnose structure independently from appearance; borrow effective hierarchy, ordering, and calls to action even from visually unattractive references. Fails when: perceived craft is itself central to the value proposition, as in premium or luxury products. ([brief-to-outcome](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=141s)) ([product-flow-system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ADaQuZS04Rc&t=10s))

## Map the complete flow

- Sketch the whole journey as labelled states, screens, branches, and arrows before detailing any screen; record where each state comes from and where it can lead. Fails when: the work is genuinely isolated to one screen or component with no branching behavior. ([flow-completeness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=23s)) ([product-flow-system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ADaQuZS04Rc&t=122s))
- Walk the map as the typical user, a user whose answer falls outside presets, and a user with no answer; require a valid forward or exit path from every state. ([flow-completeness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=23s))
- Treat each feature as a system of connected states, including empty, loading, populated, partial, success, error, disabled, and no-results states. Fails when: a static campaign or brand surface truly has no meaningful state model. ([product-flow-system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ADaQuZS04Rc&t=10s)) ([flow-completeness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=23s))
- Give a focused screen one job and remove modules that do not support it; allocate substantial new jobs to new destinations. Fails when: the screen is intentionally an aggregation hub, feed, browse surface, or mixed-intent entry point. ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=210s))
- Keep recommendations, promotions, recent items, and other secondary modules below the primary content of editors, detail views, and settings. Fails when: discovery or recommendation is the screen’s actual job. ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=210s))
- Break multi-variable tasks into a sequence of small decisions and design every handoff, confirmation, reversal, and cancellation. Fails when: the action has one trivial variable and decomposition would create needless steps. ([product-flow-system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ADaQuZS04Rc&t=122s))
- Before an irreversible batchable action, ask whether the user wants to include similar targets. Fails when: each action is personalized, sensitive, or risky enough that batching would invite mistakes. ([product-flow-system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ADaQuZS04Rc&t=122s))
- After creating an empty container, continue into the action that makes it useful, such as adding content or inviting collaborators. Provide an explicit Skip when that continuation is optional. Fails when: creation already yields a complete object, or the follow-up is legally, financially, or operationally mandatory. ([product-flow-system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ADaQuZS04Rc&t=122s))
- Audit the sequence for every point where the interface returns control without a suggested next step, recovery, or exit; treat each as a dead end. ([product-flow-system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ADaQuZS04Rc&t=122s))
- Study several shipped implementations step by step, noting where each journey could have ended but deliberately continued. Copy conventions only after checking that their constraints match yours. ([product-flow-system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ADaQuZS04Rc&t=122s)) ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=210s)) ([progressive-disclosure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ksx9C2-3yMo&t=144s))

## Allocate content and screens

- Remove pages whose only purpose is to send users to one other page; merge their useful content into the destination. Fails when: choosing among several destinations is itself the page’s purpose. ([site-architecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=83s))
- Make the product page the root of a genuinely single-product site. Fails when: the organization has multiple product lines, audiences, or substantial corporate, recruiting, investor, or editorial needs. ([site-architecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=83s))
- Preserve access to secondary destinations through persistent navigation when consolidating pages. ([site-architecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=83s))
- Preserve the existing content sequence as the redesign baseline and change it only for a stated reason. Fails when: the diagnosed problem is the existing sequence itself. ([site-architecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=83s))
- Restore narrative depth after consolidation with substantive mission, editorial, evidence, or supporting content. Fails when: the additions are filler that lengthens the journey without rewarding it. ([site-architecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=83s))
- Design read views first, then audit each for implied actions and assign every action an appropriate destination or overlay. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=280s))
- Make layouts extensible with repeatable slots for future options while preserving hierarchy. Fails when: a generic grid makes every option appear equally important. ([configuration-hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PDcQJOPby1k&t=134s))

## Build comprehensible navigation

- Treat navigation as a comprehension surface for first-time visitors, not merely a list of routes. Fails when: the product is an internal or expert tool whose returning users benefit more from compact familiar labels. ([visual-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=457s))
- Use short, concrete labels; pair them with conventional icons when those icons remain understandable without text. Fails when: no recognizable icon exists—a vague glyph is worse than text alone. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=21s))
- Give branded, invented, or technical destination names enough imagery or descriptive context for users to predict what lies behind them. Omit enrichment for universal utility labels such as Pricing, Login, or Contact. ([visual-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=457s)) ([enriched-dropdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=407s))
- Use actual, recognizable whole-object thumbnails when imagery identifies a product; favor visual choice only when appearance is genuinely decision-relevant. Fails when: compatibility, specifications, or price matter more than appearance. ([visual-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=457s))
- Group more than roughly five to seven destinations into small, labelled task clusters; move low-frequency settings and help destinations to a separated bottom group. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=21s))
- Nest growing hierarchies behind expandable groups, but keep frequent destinations at the top level. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=21s))
- Give the current route a persistent, unmistakable active state. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=21s))
- Decide whether navigation collapses before defining link anatomy, icons, ordering, or secondary blocks; design expanded and collapsed states together. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=21s)) ([dashboard-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yr2uIcFZDDQ&t=40s))
- Keep global navigation, account access, workspace switching, and global search in a stable shell rather than repeating them per page. Fails when: narrow layouts, wide data tables, or content-first pages require a top or bottom navigation pattern instead. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=21s)) ([dashboard-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yr2uIcFZDDQ&t=40s))
- Use leftover sidebar space for at most one deliberate ambient block, such as search, notifications, or a relevant promotion. Fails when: navigation already fills the column or the added block competes with wayfinding. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=21s))
- Scale dropdown architecture to the catalogue: use compact enriched panels for small sites and full-width, grouped panels only for genuinely large surfaces. ([enriched-dropdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=407s))
- Lay out enriched dropdowns as structured surfaces with explicit grouping and hierarchy, not as unformatted link columns. ([enriched-dropdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=407s))
- Add decorative dropdown imagery only when the menu has enough room and atmosphere supports comprehension or product appeal. Fails when: the navigation is compact or utility-focused and the image identifies nothing. ([visual-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=457s))
- For chaptered, full-screen narratives, provide clearly named chapters and a persistent low-profile picker for non-linear movement. Fails when: the content is short, categories overlap, or bottom controls conflict with gestures or primary actions. ([recap-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=goWOAFqJHpA&t=157s))

## Design search and choice paths

- Treat falling performance in a large catalogue as a findability problem first: improve search and reorganize taxonomy around customer language before restyling pages. Fails when: the catalogue is small enough to scan in one or two steps and trust or presentation is the true bottleneck. ([brief-to-outcome](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=141s))
- Ask only for query parameters that narrow or materially alter results. Fails when: an additional field is required by law or operations. ([intent-first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=26s))
- Pair large browse surfaces with filters that reflect users’ real constraints. Fails when: the result set is small or tightly curated enough that filtering adds more effort than it removes. ([intent-first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=26s))
- Place refinement controls with the search or collection they modify. Separate destructive or high-risk actions to prevent accidental activation. ([flow-completeness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=23s))
- Give every curated choice list an escape for valid answers outside the presets, such as search, free entry, or Other. Fails when: the set is genuinely closed and exhaustive. ([flow-completeness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=23s))
- Give optional data-collection steps an explicit null path such as Skip, None, or I don’t have one. Fails when: the information is required for function, safety, payment, or law—explain the requirement instead. ([flow-completeness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=23s))
- Design “no content yet” and “no results” as different states with different explanations and actions. ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=387s))
- Build no-results states from an acknowledgement, the user’s query, useful alternatives when available, and an explicit way to clear the search, reset filters, or return to all content. Use imagery only when it does not crowd useful information. ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=387s))
- Echo or safely truncate the actual query so users can spot mistakes and verify what the system searched. ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=387s))

## Choose the right container

- Decide first whether the user can afford to leave the current context; then escalate by task complexity and permanence from inline or popover, to sheet or modal, to a full route. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=280s)) ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=210s)) ([progressive-disclosure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ksx9C2-3yMo&t=144s))
- Use a popover for a few reversible, independently applied controls and allow outside-click dismissal. Fails when: fields depend on one another, require validation, or culminate in an irreversible commit. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=280s))
- Use a modal for a focused, consequential task whose subject remains anchored to the current screen; require explicit commit or cancel and protect entered data from accidental outside-click dismissal. Fails when: the content is trivial and read-only, unrelated to the current view, or large enough to deserve a route. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=280s))
- Name a modal’s action explicitly and provide a route to likely sibling flows, such as switching between sign-up and login. ([auth-forms](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=400s))
- Confirm an overlay’s result after it closes when the underlying change is not immediately visible. Avoid redundant confirmation when the changed content itself provides clear feedback. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=280s))
- Use a bottom sheet for a short mobile sub-task that returns a value to the current screen. Fails when: the task is long, multi-step, or a destination in its own right. ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=210s))
- Size a sheet to its content; for large pickers include a title, search, explicit confirm and dismiss actions, and scrollable content. Keep small action sheets correspondingly simple. ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=210s))
- Make touch sheets dismissible and adjustable by gesture as well as by an explicit control. ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=210s))
- Let frequent, lightweight creation begin in place through a direct input or small choice menu. Fails when: creation requires substantial structured input. ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=210s))
- Promote detailed, long-lived work to a dedicated route with a shareable URL, history semantics, and an explicit back path or breadcrumb. Fails when: a short two-field task would incur needless navigation. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=280s))
- Use transient messages for non-blocking confirmations, warnings, and background failures; use persistent inline or blocking treatment for errors users must resolve before continuing. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=280s))

## Apply progressive disclosure

- Rank actions by frequency and importance, then make primary actions persistent, secondary actions interaction-revealed, and rare actions available in settings. Fails when: hiding would harm accessibility, legal comprehension, safety, or discoverability of a destructive action. ([progressive-disclosure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ksx9C2-3yMo&t=144s))
- Tune prominence along an explicitness scale—revealed icon, visible icon, labelled icon, dedicated button—instead of treating visibility as binary. Fails when: the revealing interaction does not exist on the target device. ([progressive-disclosure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ksx9C2-3yMo&t=144s))
- Reapply hierarchy inside every menu, popover, sheet, and modal; place its single primary action first and demote supporting actions. ([progressive-disclosure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ksx9C2-3yMo&t=144s))
- Keep each row’s most frequent action directly available and reveal secondary actions through pointer- or touch-appropriate interactions. Provide a discoverable cue and name ambiguous icon-only actions. Fails when: the pattern relies on hover on touch devices or undersignals a destructive action. ([progressive-disclosure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ksx9C2-3yMo&t=144s))
- Collapse rarely used configuration under Advanced by default while keeping primary-task settings visible. Fails when: Advanced becomes a dumping ground for settings users need frequently or must find to finish. ([configuration-hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PDcQJOPby1k&t=134s))
- Promote content users need every session from a hidden header action to a persistent section. Fails when: the content is long, contextual, or occasional enough to starve the primary view. ([dashboard-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yr2uIcFZDDQ&t=40s))
- Preserve every capability outside one-time onboarding; progressive disclosure may defer discovery but must never make dismissal permanently remove access. ([progressive-disclosure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ksx9C2-3yMo&t=144s))

## Design onboarding and first-run states

- Use the empty state as contextual onboarding: explain the feature’s purpose and point to the first useful action instead of presenting a blank container. ([product-flow-system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ADaQuZS04Rc&t=10s)) ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=387s))
- Prefer one dominant first-run invitation anchored to the product’s persistent create control. Fails when: users must genuinely choose among equally important paths such as importing or starting fresh. ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=387s))
- Teach feature-dense products through one contextual prompt at a time or a persistent checklist, not a welcome modal that explains everything at once. Fails when: the interface is already self-evident and coaching would obstruct use. ([progressive-disclosure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ksx9C2-3yMo&t=144s))
- Use a short coach mark only on first run; do not turn it into a repeated or compulsory tour. ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=387s))
- Show representative or personalized value before asking users for additional setup effort. Fails when: the preview is empty, misleading, or delays required setup without demonstrating value. ([financial-onboarding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jSxxAFxjxbU&t=262s))
- Pair sensitive account-connection requests with concrete security, privacy, consent, and misuse protections. Fails when: reassurance is vague or substitutes for actual safeguards. ([financial-onboarding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jSxxAFxjxbU&t=262s))
- Split several concise onboarding insights across a discoverable sequence when they need not be compared simultaneously. Fails when: side-by-side comparison is essential or swipe navigation would be hidden. ([financial-onboarding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jSxxAFxjxbU&t=262s))
- Capture the baseline and timeframe needed to make a measurable goal actionable. Use bounded adjustment controls only when approximation is acceptable, and retain precise accessible entry where needed. Fails when: the goal is not time-bound or requires a wider, more flexible model. ([financial-onboarding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jSxxAFxjxbU&t=262s))

## Cover sharing and summaries

- Consolidate important metrics into a prioritized summary rather than duplicating every underlying screen. ([recap-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=goWOAFqJHpA&t=157s))
- Provide one primary sharing entry point, then let users choose among natural scopes such as item, section, or complete experience. ([recap-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=goWOAFqJHpA&t=360s))
- Identify the original owner prominently on shared personalized or aggregate views so recipients cannot mistake the content for their own. ([recap-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=goWOAFqJHpA&t=360s))

## Audit before handoff

- Review structure before styling: verify screen allocation, content hierarchy, navigation, actions, and flow coverage before evaluating surface craft. ([site-architecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=83s)) ([configuration-hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PDcQJOPby1k&t=134s))
- Audit every screen for navigation and exits, non-default states, action availability, and clear system outcomes; hand off local control styling and transition behavior to the appropriate design pass. ([flow-completeness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=23s))
- Remove elements before rearranging them, but verify their purpose across roles, permissions, and states before cutting. ([dashboard-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yr2uIcFZDDQ&t=40s))
- Remove duplicate controls only when their scopes truly match; if they differ, clarify their labels and placement instead. ([dashboard-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yr2uIcFZDDQ&t=40s))
- Remove labels that merely restate self-evident content. Fails when: the title is the user’s only orientation cue in a multi-context shell. ([dashboard-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yr2uIcFZDDQ&t=40s))
- Remove domain-implausible affordances that users do not need. Never remove accessibility features merely because they seem unusual for the domain. ([dashboard-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yr2uIcFZDDQ&t=40s))
- Check where the initial viewport ends and reclaim space from chrome when priority content is pushed below it. Fails when: scrolling and a substantial entry region are intentional parts of long-form or feed consumption. ([dashboard-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yr2uIcFZDDQ&t=40s))
- Represent collections as collections and expose their management path; do not let a single sample item imply that a plural feature is singular. Fails when: the entity truly is singular, such as the current account owner. ([dashboard-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yr2uIcFZDDQ&t=40s))
- Test forms and flows in filled, partial, invalid, interrupted, and submitted states rather than judging only pristine empty mockups. Preserve labels after entry and show concrete format examples only where useful. Fails when: a lone, universally understood search field needs no persistent label. ([auth-forms](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=400s))
- Audit five to ten comparable products, identify both conventions and category clichés, and deliberately decide which to retain. Preserve conventions that reduce uncertainty; reject copied structures that do not fit the product’s scale or intent. ([site-architecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=83s)) ([enriched-dropdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=407s))

## Judge these tensions explicitly

- Choose the sidebar’s top anchor according to product context: prioritize an account or workspace switcher in frequently switched multi-workspace tools, but prioritize brand identity in marketing-adjacent or strongly branded products. Do not average the two into a crowded header. ([dashboard-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY&t=21s)) ([dashboard-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yr2uIcFZDDQ&t=40s))
- Choose persistent versus disclosed placement by actual frequency: keep daily, glanceable information visible; disclose occasional or contextual material. Revisit the choice when evidence contradicts the assumed frequency. ([dashboard-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yr2uIcFZDDQ&t=40s)) ([progressive-disclosure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ksx9C2-3yMo&t=144s))
- Reconcile “one job per screen” with mixed-intent entry points by distinguishing focused task screens from hubs, dashboards, browse surfaces, and landing pages. ([mobile-focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gfsd8NNuD9g&t=210s)) ([intent-first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=26s))
- Choose enriched imagery only when it improves identification, prediction, appeal, or appropriate atmosphere; otherwise prefer compact text navigation. ([visual-navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=457s)) ([enriched-dropdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=407s))
- Preserve existing information order to control redesign risk unless flow evidence shows that the order is the problem; then change it deliberately and document why. ([site-architecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=83s))
