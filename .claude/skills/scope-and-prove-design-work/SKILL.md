## Scope the engagement around outcomes

- Diagnose the outcome behind the requested artifact before defining deliverables. Ask what must change for the business or user; treat “we need a website” as an initial symptom. Fails when the artifact itself is the legitimate outcome, as with fixed-mandate, editorial, internal, or speculative work. ([business outcomes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=0s))

- Define the problem as a specific audience, context, and desired outcome. Validate it before proposing a solution, using synthesized research rather than assertion. Fails when the evidence is biased, irrelevant, or too broad to guide decisions. ([problem validation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7mpEDXzjCg&t=44s))

- Start lightweight research with a questionnaire when formal research is impractical; cluster responses into recurring themes before deriving the problem statement. Treat convenient participants as exploratory input, not representative validation. ([problem validation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7mpEDXzjCg&t=44s))

- Choose success measures during discovery. Frame commercial problems as specific conversion leaks where a funnel exists; use task efficiency, perception, retention, or another appropriate outcome elsewhere. Fails when a conversion frame misrepresents brand, editorial, or internal-tool work. ([case-study metrics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=320s))

- Separate the buyer’s preferences from the end user’s success. Resolve conflicts in favor of user task completion unless aesthetic status or brand expression is itself the product’s function. ([client and user](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=81s))

- Require both credible presentation and functional effectiveness. For utilities, verify fast and correct task completion before polishing; for trust-sensitive or discovery-led work, treat visual credibility as a prerequisite. Do not use beauty to conceal usability defects. **Repeatedly supported.** ([business outcomes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=0s)) ([client and user](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=81s)) ([aesthetic evidence](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=438s))

## Choose and defend the pricing model

- Choose commercial terms whose incentives align with the client’s: both parties should benefit from efficient delivery and successful outcomes. Avoid arrangements that reward you for taking longer while rewarding the client for reducing hours. ([rate foundations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=22s))

- Use hourly pricing as an early-career or uncertainty scaffold. In US-comparable high-cost markets, consider roughly $20–30/hour at 1–2 years, $35–50 after several successful engagements, and $60–100 after sustaining that level for about another year. Rebase these tiers against local designer salaries instead of importing the dollar figures globally. ([rate foundations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=22s))

- Raise rates when completed engagements prove stronger capability, not merely because time passed. Point to successful delivery when defending each increase. ([rate foundations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=22s))

- Move from hourly to fixed pricing once you can estimate comparable work reliably. Keep hourly pricing for genuinely open-ended discovery, retainers, first-of-kind work, or unstable scope. ([rate foundations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=22s))

- Default to a fixed project fee when value pricing does not qualify. Give the client one hard total rather than a rate plus an open-ended estimate. Fails when scope is too uncertain to price honestly. ([rate foundations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=22s)) ([value pricing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=177s))

- Calculate the fixed-fee floor as realistic delivery hours multiplied by your internal hourly rate. Include meetings, administration, feedback, and every planned revision—not only production time. Inflate the first time estimate to counter routine optimism. Fails when the hour estimate rests on fictional or unstable scope. ([fixed fees](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=92s)) ([value pricing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=177s))

- Use published duration benchmarks only until your own tracked history exists. Replace external averages with actual project data after every engagement. ([fixed fees](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=92s))

- Quote roughly 1–2 times the break-even floor according to the engagement’s strategic value. Use about 1.5 times for an ordinary project; move lower for verified portfolio or referral value and higher for tedious, risky, or undesirable work. Reject unsupported promises of exposure or future work. ([fixed fees](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=92s))

- Keep fixed-fee hour calculations and multipliers private so the client evaluates the agreed output and budget certainty. ([fixed fees](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=92s))

- Qualify value-based pricing by requiring an existing revenue baseline and a defensible connection between the work and additional revenue. Otherwise use fixed scope. Fails for pre-revenue, nonprofit, internal, or loosely attributable work. ([value pricing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=177s))

- Build a value fee from the client’s figures: baseline annual revenue → defensible projected lift → your 5–10% share of that lift → fee. Agree on the forecast and the return the client expects before quoting. Fails when the client will not share inputs or the projected lift cannot be defended. ([value pricing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=177s))

- Show the assumption chain for value pricing so stakeholders can audit it. **Tension:** expose this arithmetic because it establishes the fee’s basis, while keeping fixed-fee time math private because it invites negotiation over inputs rather than outcomes. ([fixed fees](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=92s)) ([value pricing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=177s))

- Prefer value pricing as evidence and client scale grow, because price can track created value rather than delivery time. Fails with small clients whose plausible upside would underpay the work. ([value pricing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rtsoM2Dqrs&t=177s))

## Structure the engagement to produce proof

- Build measurement into the proposal: agree on metrics, secure access, record the baseline, ship, wait for a comparable window, and report the change. Retrofitting evidence after launch is usually impossible. ([analytics plan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=379s))

- Negotiate analytics access before work begins and continuing view-only or admin access before the engagement ends. Respect privacy and data-handling restrictions that prevent third-party access. **Repeatedly supported.** ([case-study metrics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=320s)) ([analytics plan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=379s))

- Capture pre-change metrics and compare the same measures across equivalent post-launch windows. Disclose seasonality, advertising changes, launches, publicity, or other confounders. Do not invent a baseline for a new property. ([analytics plan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=379s))

- Accept basic site analytics for credible directional evidence on small engagements. Do not use them for attribution, segmentation, cohort, or causal claims they cannot support. ([case-study metrics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=320s))

- Select metrics by the job: conversion for sales and signup paths, completion time for utilities or support, and dwell time only where sustained attention is desirable. **Tension:** longer sessions can indicate engagement or difficulty; judge direction from user intent. ([analytics plan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=379s)) ([aesthetic evidence](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=438s))

- Treat small-sample pre-launch testing as directional evidence, not proof of business impact. Continue using small qualitative studies to expose blocking usability problems without making statistical claims. ([analytics plan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=379s))

- Define implementation ownership explicitly. For small clients without technical staff, offer or coordinate an end-to-end engagement when capability permits; for complex systems or established engineering teams, preserve specialist ownership and the existing delivery pipeline. ([case-study metrics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=320s)) ([delivery structure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=379s))

- Offer a legitimate post-launch service—maintenance, monitoring, or iteration—when continued access and measurement are valuable. ([analytics plan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=379s))

## Present design as a business argument

- Explain each consequential decision through the outcome it produces for the business and its customers, not through personal taste. Preserve creative-point-of-view language for engagements explicitly buying art direction or identity. ([client and user](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=81s))

- Name documented design effects when they support the argument and let stakeholders verify them. Use evidence to make visual work defensible, not to disguise taste as certainty. ([aesthetic evidence](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=438s))

- Use a recognizable commercial example to establish a shared reference point when taste debates stall. Explain why its principle transfers; do not import the constraints, demand, or budget of a global brand into a small client’s context. ([business outcomes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=0s))

- Reject aesthetics-versus-performance as the default framing and search for a solution that serves both. Accept a real trade-off when accessibility, performance, platform, or task constraints require one. ([business outcomes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=0s))

- Present projected revenue as a mechanism and assumption, not a guarantee. Fails when traffic, product quality, sales operations, or other external factors dominate the result. ([business outcomes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=0s))

## Position the designer

- Write positioning as “I help [specific segment] achieve [specific outcome].” Replace general-purpose labels with a buyer, recurring problem, and result the buyer recognizes. Fails when the segment is too small or unsupported by any relevant work. ([client and user](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=81s))

- Choose a niche where genuine interest intersects with clients who sell worthwhile products and possess real budgets. Either interest or commercial viability alone is insufficient. ([self-initiated work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=59XWYgN00nQ&t=0s))

- Derive specialization from recurring operational problems observed in real engagements, not merely an attractive industry label. Do not generalize an atypical client’s constraints to the entire market. ([client and user](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=81s))

- Make the portfolio coherent around one niche when seeking specialist clients. **Tension:** retain breadth while exploring or pursuing generalist in-house roles; do not let premature specialization become a cage. ([self-initiated work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=59XWYgN00nQ&t=0s))

## Create credible portfolio work

- Create self-initiated projects when client work is unavailable. Give each one a real brief, constraints, rationale, and coherent outcome; never present an arbitrary polished screen as a solved problem. ([self-initiated work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=59XWYgN00nQ&t=0s))

- Start speculative work from an external prompt, then expand it into audience, goals, tone, deliverables, and constraints before designing. Edit any LLM-generated brief until it becomes specific enough to judge decisions against. ([self-initiated work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=59XWYgN00nQ&t=0s))

- Sequence speculative work as niche → written brief → multi-source references → design. Gather references after the brief so the problem filters the inspiration rather than the trend defining the problem. Relax this sequence only for deliberate style exploration. ([self-initiated work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=59XWYgN00nQ&t=0s))

- Fill speculative artifacts with plausible domain-specific names, prices, labels, and microcopy. Never use placeholder Latin where content affects credibility or layout. ([portfolio publishing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=59XWYgN00nQ&t=72s))

- Present self-initiated work on equal footing with commissioned work without apologizing for it. Never claim a client relationship, launch, or outcome that did not exist. ([portfolio publishing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=59XWYgN00nQ&t=72s))

- Build a reference library from several admired professional portfolios before designing your own. Borrow proven structural conventions while making the work and personality distinctive. ([portfolio candor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=387s))

- Choose the publishing container by the evidence: use a shot-oriented platform for isolated visuals, a long-form platform for case studies, and Notion or a custom site when narrative and personality matter. Treat a custom site as the target but publish through a simpler container when building it would delay the work. ([portfolio publishing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=59XWYgN00nQ&t=72s))

- Limit an early portfolio to two or three strong case studies, or three or four artifact-led pieces. Remove anything that lowers the perceived standard; do not let perfectionism prevent a beginner from publishing enough work to evaluate. **Repeatedly supported.** ([portfolio publishing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=59XWYgN00nQ&t=72s)) ([hiring lens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6CC8lLnqa28&t=113s))

## Build case studies for the buyer

- Choose the format from what the buyer must evaluate. Use full case studies when reasoning is the product; lead with artifacts when final craft supplies the evidence. Restore process for strategy, systems, research, or roles with explicit format requirements. ([case-study fit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=265s))

- Structure each case study around services or role, validated problem, solution, and measured outcome. Make both the reasoning and your prospective value evident. **Repeatedly supported.** ([case-study metrics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=320s)) ([case-study value](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7mpEDXzjCg&t=0s))

- Place a short, plain-language solution summary immediately after the problem so a skimming reviewer can understand the result. Avoid exposing confidential information or implying exploratory work was final. ([problem validation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7mpEDXzjCg&t=44s))

- Attach at least one defensible number to the outcome when before-and-after measurement exists. State the window and confounders; omit or qualify cherry-picked, short-window, or misleading figures. ([case-study metrics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=320s))

- Keep first-contact versions to a short problem–solution–outcome outline. **Tension:** expand a reasoning-led hiring case study into a coherent 10–15-minute read when the reviewer needs process depth; use longer formats only where RFP, award, or evaluation context rewards them. ([case-study metrics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=320s)) ([case-study narrative](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7mpEDXzjCg&t=544s))

- Tell meaningful discoveries, constraints, setbacks, and decisions in the order they changed the work. Do not manufacture drama or include routine friction unrelated to judgment. ([case-study narrative](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7mpEDXzjCg&t=544s))

- Use descriptive, chapter-like headings that reveal each section’s development instead of generic labels such as “Process.” ([case-study narrative](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7mpEDXzjCg&t=544s))

- Treat case-study presentation as part of the evidence. Keep prose concise and interleave each major idea with a relevant still, prototype, recording, or diagram; remove decorative filler. **Repeatedly supported.** ([case-study presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7mpEDXzjCg&t=152s)) ([portfolio story](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=204s)) ([case-study narrative](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7mpEDXzjCg&t=544s))

- Match the dominant medium to the work being sold. Let artifacts lead for visual work, but retain enough explanation to expose reasoning in research- or decision-heavy roles. ([portfolio story](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=204s))

- Show final artifacts large enough to inspect and pair each with nearby copy explaining the decision and problem solved. Use progressive disclosure for secondary rationale without hiding essential evidence. ([case-study decisions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7mpEDXzjCg&t=406s))

- Use motion for states, transitions, and interactions that a still cannot explain. Limit autoplaying loops when they harm page speed, concentration, or readability. ([portfolio story](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=204s))

- Test the visual narrative by scrolling without reading. For visual work, the sequence should still communicate problem, development, and outcome; reasoning-heavy disciplines may require text or diagrams to lead. ([portfolio story](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=204s))

- End with a short reflection naming consequential limitations, one decision you would revisit, what you learned, and prioritized future work. Acknowledge weak sampling explicitly. Avoid vague apologies, exhaustive flaw lists, or critiques that invalidate the project. **Repeatedly supported.** ([portfolio story](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=204s)) ([case-study fit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=265s)) ([case-study reflection](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7mpEDXzjCg&t=459s))

## Shape the portfolio and hiring narrative

- Put your name, role, and a real photo in the first viewport. Keep the intro to those essential facts; use a short loop only when you appear comfortable on camera, otherwise use a still against a simple background. ([portfolio hero](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=0s))

- Lead visual project entries with large imagery and subordinate captions through shorter, smaller, lower-contrast copy. Use high-impact color only when it matches the work’s identity; let reasoning lead when screenshots cannot demonstrate the value. ([portfolio scrolling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=145s))

- Keep the portfolio architecture predictable: home, contact, and an optional about page are usually sufficient. Fold biography into the homepage until it genuinely requires its own route. Break conventions deliberately, not as a reason to skip clarity. ([portfolio structure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=84s))

- Use one scrolling page with anchor navigation when the body of work is small. **Tension:** give deep case studies separate shareable URLs when a single page becomes long, disorienting, or unsuitable for search and review. ([portfolio scrolling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=145s))

- Write personal material in first person and plain speech rather than restating the résumé. Skip a separate about page when you have nothing specific or human to add. ([case-study fit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=265s))

- Add memorable human details: concrete enthusiasms, harmlessly opinionated tastes, and a real photo. Make them specific enough to be retold, but never trade professional safety or other people’s privacy for memorability. ([portfolio personality](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=327s)) ([portfolio candor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=387s))

- Place human material on the main scroll where visitors can encounter it after the work. Use a navigation anchor when page length would otherwise bury it. ([portfolio personality](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=327s))

- Maintain a dated personal update only if you can keep it current. Include honest setbacks and mundane events as well as wins, without airing grievances, exposing others, or oversharing. ([portfolio personality](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=327s))

- Acknowledge a stale portfolio plainly when you cannot refresh it before review. Keep the note secondary to substantive work, and avoid this tactic when a formal screening rubric will treat recency as a hard requirement. ([portfolio candor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ToJiXPTNnLY&t=387s))

- Reframe existing work around the specific employer’s criteria rather than submitting a neutral highlight reel. Preserve accuracy and enough generality for reuse. ([hiring lens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6CC8lLnqa28&t=113s))

- Translate self-taught or freelance work into the vocabulary a structured reviewer expects: problem, constraints, process, contribution, and outcome. Do not wrap shallow work in process boilerplate. ([hiring lens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6CC8lLnqa28&t=113s))

- Review the résumé and portfolio in one pass from the hiring manager’s seat and answer, “Would I hire this person for this role?” Convert every hesitation into a named evidence gap and concrete revision rather than a confidence judgment. ([hiring lens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6CC8lLnqa28&t=113s))

## Boundary and evidence

This skill produces commercial positioning, pricing, engagement structure, proof plans, portfolios, résumés, case studies, and hiring narratives. Interface construction and product-analytics visualization remain outside its scope.
