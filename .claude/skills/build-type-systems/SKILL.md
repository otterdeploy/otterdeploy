## Build in this order

1. **Inventory and rank every text role before styling it.** Identify body, headings, subheadings, labels, values, captions, metadata, links, and display moments; order them by communicative importance. ([role hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=335s))
2. **Choose the scale width from information density.** Use a broad, dramatic range for persuasive pages and a compressed range for dashboards and tools. Fails when: one design system spans both marketing and product surfaces; define separate scales that share a family instead. ([UI typography](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=196s))
3. **Select and license the typefaces before polishing layout.** Then assign each family a non-overlapping role. Fails when: an established product system fixes the type stack already; preserve it and let layout lead. ([type-led layout](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=151s))
4. **Generate sizes systematically, then tune spacing and apparent ink for each role.** Validate the complete system in real layouts rather than approving isolated token values. ([modular scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=427s), [hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=10s))
5. **Review the result at actual size on its target device class.** Check hierarchy, reading comfort, contrast, wrapping, and rendering without relying on a zoomed design canvas. ([leading](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=688s))

## Select typefaces by function

- **Choose a category from the text’s job, then test the choice in context.** Start with sans-serif for interfaces, serif for sustained reading, and display or handwritten faces for short oversized text. Fails when: an editorial product deliberately inverts these conventions, or real-device tests show that a screen-optimized serif works well in UI. ([type categories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=30s))
- **Use historical associations as hypotheses, not truths.** Test serif when the product should feel established or authoritative and sans-serif when it should feel current or utilitarian. Fails when: the audience reads those associations differently, or the specific face contradicts its category stereotype. ([type categories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=30s))
- **Test body serifs at the smallest production size before committing.** Confirm that fine terminals survive the actual renderer and display density. Fails when: none; the claimed legibility advantage disappears when those details do not render. ([type categories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=30s))
- **Default interface work to one neutral, well-tested sans-serif family.** Spend more effort on hierarchy and spacing than on auditioning interchangeable grotesques. Fails when: brand differentiation depends on typography, a mandated brand face exists, long-form editorial reading benefits from another family, or code and tabular content require monospace. This one-family default is corroborated across sources. ([pairing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=190s), [UI typography](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=196s))
- **Maintain a small library of three or four families you know thoroughly.** Reuse it for supporting typography instead of restarting an open-ended search on every project. Fails when: creating a distinctive identity is central to the assignment; habitual choices can make unrelated brands look alike. ([font sourcing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=153s))
- **Avoid professionally stigmatized novelty faces unless the context explicitly needs them.** Treat Comic Sans as unsuitable for normal professional and portfolio work. Fails when: the work is deliberately playful, ironic, child-oriented, or accessibility testing shows that its irregular forms materially help the audience. ([font sourcing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=153s))

## Source and license fonts

- **Bound discovery to a curated, license-clear source or shortlist.** Filter by the required category instead of browsing an unlimited catalogue. Fails when: the brand requires a proprietary face or the free platform’s terms do not cover the intended distribution. ([font sourcing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=153s), [display type](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=285s))
- **Reverse-engineer strong reference sites when you need direction quickly.** Inspect rendered text and network-loaded font files to identify families already proven in context. Fails when: identification is mistaken for permission to copy a paid, bespoke, or self-hosted font. ([type-led layout](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=151s))
- **Verify licensing before embedding or distributing any font.** Check commercial use, web embedding, self-hosting, application distribution, and redistribution separately; buy the correct licence or substitute a permitted face. “Free for personal use” is not a commercial licence. ([font sourcing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=153s), [type-led layout](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=151s))
- **Study shipped examples of the same interface or section pattern before designing it.** Extract conventions and constraints, not merely surface styling. Fails when: the reference product serves a different audience, density, or content model. ([UI typography](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=196s))

## Pair and assign families

- **Use one family until a concrete role requires another.** Cap normal sites at two families. Permit a third only when it owns a small, distinct category; treat four as unsupported unless exceptional evidence proves coherence. Fails when: an intentional editorial or brand system requires a proven multi-family composition. ([pairing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=190s), [UI typography](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=196s))
- **Pair by obvious role contrast.** Prefer display or script plus sans-serif, or serif plus sans-serif, so hierarchy reads as intentional. Fails when: two same-category faces are similar enough to look like an accidental mismatch rather than distinct roles. ([pairing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=190s))
- **Confine display, script, and handwritten faces to short, large text.** Vet handwritten faces for credible rhythm rather than superficial imitation. Never assign these categories to paragraphs or small UI text. ([type categories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=30s), [pairing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=190s))
- **If a third family is necessary, reserve it for an unmistakably different role.** A monospace face can mark captions, credits, code, or technical metadata. Fails when: the third family duplicates an existing role, spreads into body copy, or monospace widths slow sustained reading. ([pairing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=190s))

## Measure and align type anatomically

- **Use only anatomy that changes implementation decisions.** Prioritize baseline, x-height, cap height, ascenders, descenders, and tracking. Fails when: doing type design, font engineering, hinting, or variable-font production; those tasks require the full vocabulary. ([anatomy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=100s))
- **Measure x-height from a flat lowercase glyph such as `x`.** Do not measure from round or pointed glyphs because optical overshoot extends them beyond the reference lines. Fails when: treating this measurement convention as permission to nudge individual overshooting letters; preserve the font’s intended outlines. ([anatomy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=100s))
- **Measure cap height from a flat capital such as `E`, `H`, or `T`.** Avoid `O`, `C`, and `S`, whose curves overshoot the cap line and baseline. ([anatomy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=100s))
- **Align visible ink using baseline, x-height, and cap height rather than trusting the font bounding box.** Apply deliberate optical offsets when aligning type with icons, avatars, or differently sized text. Fails when: expecting ordinary CSS centring to perform cap-height alignment; line boxes include font-specific leading, ascenders, and descenders, so use trimming or explicit offsets. ([anatomy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=100s))

## Build the size scale

- **Generate scale steps from one base and one fixed ratio instead of choosing every size independently.** Use a systematic scale for reusable products and sites. Fails when: the work is a single fixed composition where a reusable scale offers little value. ([modular scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=427s))
- **Start web body copy near a readable 16px base, then generate larger roles upward.** Adjust only when the chosen face, audience, or device testing provides evidence for a different base. ([modular scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=427s))
- **Choose the ratio from the number of roles and the allowable total range.** Use fewer, larger jumps when the page has few roles and smaller jumps when many roles must fit into limited space. ([modular scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=427s))
- **Use about 1.62 only for a scale with one or two jumps.** Fails when: four or more compounded steps produce an impractical top size; large poster-like or editorial typography may intentionally accept that result. ([modular scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=427s))
- **Use about 1.27 for a general four-to-six-step scale.** This keeps intermediate roles while preserving a golden-ratio relationship every two steps. ([modular scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=427s))
- **Use about 1.17 for dense interfaces and constrained mobile layouts.** Fails when: adjacent sizes become visually indistinguishable; reinforce hierarchy with weight or apparent ink instead of adding arbitrary sizes. ([modular scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=427s))
- **Compute the largest step before adopting a ratio.** Reject a mathematically tidy scale when its compounded endpoint does not fit the actual interface. Fails when: an oversized endpoint is the intentional display gesture. ([modular scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=427s))
- **Keep marketing and landing-page scales to roughly six distinct sizes.** Give each size a named role and a visibly different purpose. Fails when: deep documentation or a large content system legitimately requires more nesting levels. ([UI typography](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=196s))
- **Compress dashboard and tool typography aggressively.** Keep most text at or below roughly 24px so data, not oversized labels, remains dominant. Fails when: a hero KPI deliberately anchors the screen or accessibility and mobile legibility require a larger floor. ([UI typography](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=196s))
- **Validate a proposed scale inside real screens and compare old and new versions directly.** Accept a subtle improvement in rhythm as success; do not exaggerate the ratio merely to make the redesign conspicuous. ([modular scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=427s))

## Make the scale fluid

- **Define each responsive size at two endpoints, then interpolate continuously between them.** Use `smallSize + (largeSize - smallSize) × ((width - smallWidth) / (largeWidth - smallWidth))` rather than hand-authoring intermediate sizes. Fails when: a lockup, fixed-height control, or deliberately restructured mobile hierarchy must snap at a breakpoint. ([fluid scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=581s))
- **Choose viewport anchors from the audience’s real device range.** Use approximately 320px and 1920px only as general-purpose defaults. Fails when: embedded displays, ultrawide dashboards, or analytics show a materially different range. ([fluid scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=581s))
- **Clamp every fluid size to its designed endpoints.** Use `max(floor, formula)` to enforce the minimum and `min(ceiling, formula)` to enforce the maximum. Fails when: deliberate extrapolation beyond an endpoint supports the composition; remove that bound consciously. ([fluid scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=581s))
- **Plot size against viewport width before shipping the formula.** Inspect the curve inside and outside the anchor range instead of relying only on manual window resizing. ([fluid scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=581s))
- **Hand off mobile and desktop endpoints with explicit interpolation behavior.** Do not leave intermediate widths as undefined breakpoint guesswork. ([fluid scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=581s))
- **Keep family, role assignments, weights, and hierarchy logic stable across screens while sizes respond.** Change those core choices only when the smaller layout genuinely uses a different information hierarchy. ([role hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=335s), [fluid scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=581s))

## Set line height

- **Start running text near `1.5` line-height.** Increase it for long measures and reduce it for narrow columns or short blocks. Fails when: the column geometry makes 1.5 visibly too loose or a deliberate baseline grid requires fixed leading. ([leading](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=688s))
- **Set multiline headings around `1.1–1.3`, tightening toward `1.1` as size increases.** Override automatic leading for large display text. Fails when: the heading stays on one line, ascenders and descenders collide, or the chosen face already has unusually tight metrics. This inverse size-to-leading relationship is corroborated across sources. ([leading](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=688s), [UI typography](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=196s))
- **Assign line height per scale step rather than applying one global multiplier.** Increase the multiplier as type becomes smaller and reduce it as type becomes larger. ([leading](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=688s))
- **Express leading as a unitless ratio or percentage so it follows responsive size changes.** Fails when: the system intentionally snaps every line to an absolute baseline grid. ([leading](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=688s))

## Set tracking

- **Treat tracking as size-dependent.** Leave well-made faces at their default around normal body and UI sizes; add space only when very small text needs it, and tighten large type. Fails when: optical sizing or size-specific font masters already provide the correction. This reconciles the tension between “adjust inversely with size” and “do not disturb body defaults.” ([anatomy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=100s), [tracking](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1TvOcKdBVE&t=22s))
- **Start large headlines near `-2%` to `-3%` tracking and test up to about `-4%` above 70–80px.** Fails when: the face is already tightly fitted, the text is small, or all-caps readability requires added space. This display correction is corroborated across sources. ([UI typography](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=196s), [tracking](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1TvOcKdBVE&t=22s))
- **Express tracking relative to font size rather than as fixed pixels.** Preserve the intended optical effect across responsive scaling. ([tracking](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1TvOcKdBVE&t=22s))
- **Try tracking before rejecting a body face that looks loose when enlarged.** Fails when: the letterforms themselves break down at display size; spacing is a first diagnosis, not a guaranteed repair. ([tracking](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1TvOcKdBVE&t=22s))
- **Compare tuned and untuned versions side by side.** Approve the optical result, not merely the changed numeric value. ([tracking](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1TvOcKdBVE&t=22s))

## Control weight and apparent ink

- **Use size, weight, and text colour as a coordinated hierarchy system, but change only as many levers as the distinction needs.** Tension to judge: one strong lever often creates a cleaner two-level hierarchy, while complex or flat layouts may need multiple levers. Do not average these approaches into weak changes everywhere. ([ink hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=270s), [hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=10s))
- **Treat weight and opacity as two controls over apparent ink density at normal text sizes.** Choose either one for simple prominence changes; combine them carefully when roles require stronger separation. Fails when: large display text exposes stroke character clearly enough that thin dark and bold faded type no longer feel equivalent. ([ink hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=270s))
- **Make every hierarchical step perceptually unmistakable.** Skip at least one intermediate weight—for example, Regular to Semibold instead of Regular to Medium—and compare the rendered results. ([ink hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=270s), [hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=10s))
- **Use no more than two weights by default and select them from the readable middle of the family.** Avoid hairline and black cuts for running text. Fails when: a large editorial or marketing system needs a separate display weight, or a logotype deliberately uses an extreme cut. ([ink hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=270s), [hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=10s))
- **Keep continuously read body copy at full-strength ink and a normal text weight.** Do not make the smallest or most frequently read text the faintest. ([role hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=335s))
- **Derive secondary ink from the primary text colour at roughly 45–70% opacity.** Prefer this to an unrelated hand-picked grey, particularly across light and dark themes. Fails when: the resulting contrast is inaccessible on the actual background, over imagery, or at the rendered text size; compute contrast rather than trusting the range. ([ink hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=270s), [hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=10s))
- **Keep hierarchy colours to primary and secondary ink by default.** Exclude links, errors, warnings, and success states from this limit because their colours communicate semantics rather than rank. ([hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=10s))
- **Avoid compounding a large weight drop with a large opacity drop unless extreme separation is intentional.** Fails when: the contrast between fine print and a bold heading is deliberately very strong. ([ink hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=270s))
- **Let size establish hierarchy by itself when it already creates decisive separation.** Do not automatically make every large heading bold and differently coloured. Fails when: limited space, a compressed scale, or accessibility constraints prevent size alone from distinguishing the role. ([role hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=335s))
- **Increase subheading weight enough to support scanning while keeping paragraphs comfortable to read.** ([role hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=335s))
- **When a structurally sound layout feels flat, test stronger weight and ink contrast before changing its composition.** Fails when: the information architecture assigns prominence incorrectly; styling cannot repair the wrong role order. ([hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=10s))

## Use display typography deliberately

- **Let a short, central statement become the visual anchor when imagery would add little.** Choose a distinctive display face, enlarge it confidently, and keep competing decoration restrained. Fails when: the content is long, the text is a UI label, or the face is a neutral workhorse whose enlargement looks accidental. This technique is corroborated across sources. ([type-led layout](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=151s), [display type](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=285s))
- **Choose display faces from a deliberate shortlist instead of automatically enlarging the body family.** Fails when: the body family contains a genuinely strong display cut or brand consistency outweighs added expression. ([display type](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=285s))
- **Keep oversized display copy short enough to read as one compositional shape while remaining clear language.** Fails when: a long sentence or paragraph fragments the shape and turns reading into work. ([display type](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=285s))
- **Use one oversized display moment per page by default and never exceed two without strong justification.** Repetition removes the contrast that makes the treatment effective. Fails when: no supported exception is established; prove any departure in the full-page hierarchy. ([display type](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=285s))
- **Vary scale, weight, or family selectively down expressive pages so key statements break the paragraph rhythm.** Fails when: documentation, data-heavy tools, or dense interfaces depend on repetition for scanning. ([type-led layout](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=151s))
- **If motion supports a key display moment, animate the text’s lines or letters rather than an unrelated container.** Fails when: repeated reveals delay reading, create noise, or ignore reduced-motion preferences. ([type-led layout](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=151s))

## Validate the system

- **Test the smallest text, the longest lines, wrapped headings, all-caps labels, extreme weights, light and dark themes, and real content before approval.** Use the rendered output rather than font specimens as the decision surface. ([type categories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=30s), [leading](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=688s))
- **Audit whether each family, size, weight, colour, line height, and tracking value performs a named role.** Remove distinctions that are numerically different but perceptually indistinguishable. ([ink hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=270s), [UI typography](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=196s))
- **Keep detailed specimens, measurements, licence records, and source-by-source evidence outside the skill body.** Load the relevant material from `references/` only when auditing a font, resolving a licence, or revisiting a disputed rule. ([font sourcing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=153s), [anatomy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sUUzOCv47U&t=100s))
