## Establish the direction

- Fix weak interface craft before art-directing its presentation; staging amplifies what is already there. **Fails when:** never—presentation cannot rescue a poor underlying design. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))
- Treat art direction and final presentation as required deliverables whenever work will be judged, and budget explicit time for both. **Fails when:** the artifact is an internal working file, implementation specification, or engineering handoff where direct legibility matters more than staging. ([presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=0s))
- Start with one anchor image that already expresses the desired mood; derive the visual palette, texture, and typography from its light and atmosphere. **Fails when:** fixed brand standards, accessibility tokens, or dense utility interfaces must lead the work. ([mood system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=103s))
- Locate two or three references that bracket the intended tone, then state where the brand should sit between them. Search by expressive quality, not only by industry. **Fails when:** the references become styles to copy rather than coordinates for judgment. ([decorated heroes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SfX43uIubj4&t=22s))
- Choose a concrete metaphor—printed menu, field guide, technical manual, luxury pack—and use it to test type, spacing, imagery, and materials. **Fails when:** the metaphor is unrelated to the product and turns the identity into costume. ([mood system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=103s))
- Build the identity in dependency order: anchor image, mood palette, accents, texture, type, icons, and finally the mark. **Fails when:** an existing brand mandates a different starting point. ([mood system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=103s))
- Audit the source material for missing identity assets—name, logo, wordmark, photography, and packaging surfaces—before producing downstream artwork. **Fails when:** none; discovering missing inputs midway creates avoidable rework. ([packaging](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=78s))
- Judge every decision by whether it conveys this brand’s intended feeling, not whether it follows a fashionable recipe. **Fails when:** “feeling” is used to excuse illegibility, inaccessible contrast, or misleading product representation. ([mood system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=103s))
- Keep the visual identity separable from the underlying interface structure so another direction can be tested without rebuilding hierarchy. **Fails when:** the structure depends intrinsically on one identity’s asset shapes, proportions, or quantity. ([section treatment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=318s))
- Reject unconsidered stock-light and stock-dark aesthetics; choose a specific tonal register such as dark-warm, pale-editorial, or bright-saturated. **Fails when:** long-session reading, accessibility, or user-controlled themes take priority over a fixed branded mood. ([mood system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=103s))

## Name and mark the brand

- Choose a name that activates useful category associations before attempting cleverness. Verify foreign-language or culturally borrowed names with a knowledgeable speaker. **Fails when:** the association is inaccurate, culturally careless, offensive, or so conventional that it becomes indistinguishable. ([packaging](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=78s))
- Prefer a type-led wordmark when typography can carry the desired positioning without an unnecessary symbol. **Fails when:** the brand must remain recognizable in spaces too small or constrained for the full name.
- Audit an existing type system member by member; retain faces that already serve the brand and replace only the weak roles. **Fails when:** the entire stack is irreparably inconsistent. ([mood system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=103s))
- Use monospace for functional material such as code, terminal output, or aligned figures—not as automatic personality on consumer marketing. Pair a workhorse sans with a serif when the brand needs warmth or editorial character. **Fails when:** the category’s authentic visual language is deliberately technical. ([mood system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=103s))
- Split a display headline across two complementary faces or styles at a meaningful phrase boundary. Use the move sparingly. **Fails when:** the split is arbitrary, repeated on every heading, or weakens comprehension. ([section treatment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=318s))
- Pair loose, hand-drawn marks with rigid, high-contrast typography so personality comes from the collision of precise and imperfect forms. **Fails when:** the surrounding identity is already script-heavy, informal, or densely textured. ([hand-drawn](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=77s))
- Draw annotation marks yourself at their intended display size and maintain one consistent stroke character across circles, arrows, underlines, and asterisks. **Fails when:** the mark is intentionally made by multiple voices or tools. ([hand-drawn](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=77s))
- Attach drawn marks to meaning: circle a word, underline a phrase, or point toward an action. Emphasize one target, not everything. **Fails when:** there is no clear priority or the marks float as contextless ornament. ([hand-drawn](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=77s))
- Keep annotation layers optically behind crisp type with restrained opacity, softness, or blur. **Fails when:** the drawn mark itself is the primary content, such as a hand-lettered logo. ([hand-drawn](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=77s))
- Pair a drawing-led hero with sparse copy and substantial negative space. Budget time for placement, timing, and stroke refinement despite the style’s apparent simplicity. **Fails when:** dense documentation or long-form copy is the product. ([hand-drawn](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=77s))
- Animate hand-drawn accents where interaction supports it—draw a stroke, swap a mark, or nudge it on hover—so the authored quality extends through time. **Fails when:** motion distracts from the action or hover is unavailable without an equivalent touch behavior. ([hand-drawn](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=77s))

## Direct photography and imagery

- Treat image selection, treatment, and cropping as primary design decisions because imagery often carries more visual weight than the interface around it. **Fails when:** imagery is genuinely incidental and receives little visual area. ([imagery](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=120s))
- Choose subjects that identify the product’s real domain—the objects, environments, or actions its users encounter. **Fails when:** the product is abstract and literal photography becomes generic stock filler. ([mood system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=103s))
- Introduce photography only when it is relevant and quiet enough to support the subject. Prefer abstract branded treatments for pure software products without meaningful physical imagery. **Fails when:** the photographic context is itself the product story. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))
- Use generated imagery only when it fits the context and sourcing constraints; do not equate technical image quality with brand suitability. **Fails when:** authentic, supplied, regulated, documentary, or context-specific photography is required. ([imagery](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=120s))
- Crop a single clear subject out of its background and let it escape its container when the composition needs focus and depth. **Fails when:** the contour cannot be isolated cleanly or the surrounding context carries essential meaning. ([imagery](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=120s))
- Crop product photography tightly around a recognizable material, form, or detail when drama matters more than completeness. **Fails when:** viewers need the whole object to understand or compare the product. ([imagery](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=120s))
- Use wide environmental imagery as a message-bearing panel only after creating a quiet, high-contrast region for the copy. **Fails when:** the overlay hides important image content or cannot remain readable. ([imagery](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=120s))
- Mask imagery with silhouettes drawn from the brand’s existing shape language. **Fails when:** the mask clips important content, harms recognition, or adds novelty unsupported by the identity. ([imagery](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=120s))
- Apply one repeatable treatment chain to a photographic set—intentional crop, controlled darkening, and fine grain—to make mixed sources feel related. **Fails when:** the imagery is a product screenshot or detail image whose information would be damaged. ([mood system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=103s))
- Inspect apparently flat colour fields at full size; subtle falloff that disappears in a thumbnail may become obvious when used full-bleed. **Fails when:** none—judge imagery at its delivery scale. ([photo extension](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=322s))
- Extend a soft edge region by duplicating, vertically flipping, and cropping the original pixels before reaching for generative fill. **Fails when:** the edge contains faces, text, horizons, or recognizable geometry that makes mirroring obvious. ([photo extension](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=322s))
- When a weak supporting image cannot be replaced, reveal only its useful crop or let another surface occlude it. Reduce an asset’s prominence before spending heavily to improve it. **Fails when:** the image is decision-critical and viewers need an honest, complete view. ([photo extension](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=322s))
- Distinguish visually similar product variants through branded card treatments and explicit text rather than asking nearly identical photographs to carry the difference. **Fails when:** colour implies a false product attribute or has no defensible relationship to the variant. ([photo extension](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=322s))

## Derive a mood palette without engineering UI tokens

- Eyedrop base colours from the anchor imagery so the identity inherits one coherent lighting environment. Treat these as art-direction inputs, not production-ready accessibility tokens. **Fails when:** sampled tones lack usable contrast or conflict with established brand requirements. ([mood system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=103s))
- Sample adjacent gradients directly from the photograph they extend so hue, saturation, and colour cast continue across the seam. **Fails when:** the sampled values violate brand or text-contrast constraints; then use them only as a starting point. ([photo extension](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=322s))
- Add only two or three deliberate accents when a low-chroma image-derived palette lacks emphasis, and assign each accent a clear expressive role. **Fails when:** the number of accents exceeds the hierarchy they are meant to communicate. ([mood system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=103s))
- Pull presentation backgrounds from the approved identity palette or image library instead of inventing colours during composition. **Fails when:** a new context genuinely requires extending the identity. ([section treatment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=318s))
- Derive a restrained showcase background from the interface accent by darkening and desaturating it toward grey. **Fails when:** no meaningful accent exists or the background itself is the identity’s principal statement. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))

## Compose decoration around meaning

- Preserve decoration that helps a first-time viewer infer the product category; remove ornament that carries no semantic or tonal value. **Fails when:** a meaningful object still overwhelms the message—it must then be reduced, not blindly preserved. ([decorated heroes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SfX43uIubj4&t=22s))
- Build expressive heroes around a clear text core, arranging contextual objects around—not over—the message. Maintain a generous untouchable clear zone around all copy. **Fails when:** the assets crowd the payload or require the copy to compete for attention. ([decorated heroes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SfX43uIubj4&t=22s)) ([section treatment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=318s))
- Give scattered imagery explicit physics: place larger, heavier objects lower; smaller objects higher; and keep every object outside the text safety margin. **Fails when:** placement becomes genuinely random and loses the implied depth order. ([section treatment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=318s))
- Angle and orient peripheral objects toward the focal point, then taper their scale, density, or opacity toward the canvas edges. **Fails when:** directional elements pull attention out of the composition. ([decorated heroes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SfX43uIubj4&t=22s))
- Leave some asymmetrical whitespace untouched; do not add an object merely to fill the last opening. **Fails when:** the emptiness creates an unintended competing focal point rather than useful breathing room. ([decorated heroes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SfX43uIubj4&t=22s))
- Budget iterative, eyeballed adjustment for off-grid arrangements because automated alignment cannot resolve their optical balance. **Fails when:** dense functional interfaces need the predictability and scanning benefits of a grid. ([decorated heroes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SfX43uIubj4&t=22s))
- Match illustration style to the brand’s playful-to-professional position: characters, blobs, and saturated forms can signal play; realistic renders and photography can signal seriousness. **Fails when:** the imagery’s tone contradicts the product’s credibility needs. ([decorated heroes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SfX43uIubj4&t=22s))
- Use brand-deck icons and real product imagery in feature storytelling rather than importing a generic visual language. **Fails when:** the feature is nonvisual or the real interface is too unfinished to support the claim. ([section treatment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=318s))
- Apply a restrained vignette to busy edge-to-edge imagery when the centre must win. **Fails when:** the treatment appears on a clean, light surface as an obvious artifact. ([section treatment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=318s))

## Use texture deliberately

- Match texture to the intended feeling: fine noise for warmth, stronger grain over photography, grid texture for technical storytelling, and no texture for precise software surfaces. **Fails when:** texture becomes a default layer unrelated to the section’s job. ([section treatment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs&t=318s))
- Add fine grain to flat decorative gradients when they should feel printed, manufactured, or tactile. **Fails when:** the visual language calls for pristine digital precision. ([kaleidoscope](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=14s))
- Prototype any complex material recipe on a plain primitive before applying it to real artwork. **Fails when:** the target silhouette fundamentally changes how the effect behaves. ([clay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=76s))
- Capture dense layer recipes as a compact reference artifact after validating them so the effect can be recreated reliably. **Fails when:** the treatment is intentionally flattened and will never be edited. ([kaleidoscope](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=14s))

## Build expressive gradients and materials

- Build an organic mesh from one large, blurred, low-opacity base circle plus additional blurred circles set to Overlay. Blur each until its circular edge disappears. **Fails when:** the surface must be tokenized, reproduced mathematically, rendered cheaply, or kept crisp. ([mesh gradient](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=164s))
- Increase mesh intensity through additional overlapping layers—often eight to ten for a rich field—then tune hue, blur, and opacity by eye. Save exact values or flatten the result when others must reproduce it. **Fails when:** accumulated hotspots destroy foreground contrast. ([mesh gradient](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=164s))
- Use mesh gradients on semantically empty surfaces such as poster fields, title slides, card backs, and atmospheric heroes. **Fails when:** decoration competes with interactive controls or information. ([mesh gradient](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=164s))
- Construct a kaleidoscope with a radial base, an even-segment black-and-white angular gradient set to Difference, and a duplicate angular layer set to Screen at the top of the stack. Start with 6, 8, or 10 segments. **Fails when:** another layer is placed above the Screen composite or the effect must remain quiet and legible. ([kaleidoscope](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=14s))
- Treat blend-mode stacking order as part of the asset’s specification, not an incidental layer arrangement. **Fails when:** the effect has been flattened intentionally. ([kaleidoscope](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=14s))
- Integrate display type into a chromatic surface with heavy weight and Overlay only when decoration is the primary job. **Fails when:** the text is body copy, a label, a control, or anything requiring stable contrast. ([kaleidoscope](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=14s))
- Build a soft clay material from two matched inner shadows with equal blur and spread but inverted offsets, then add an optional soft cast shadow. Reuse the recipe across the set. **Fails when:** shapes are too small, strokes are thin, or opposing shadows merge into mud. ([clay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=76s))
- Use muted fills for clay so the shadowed rim reads as the same material. **Fails when:** saturated colour leaves insufficient tonal range for believable shading. ([clay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=76s))
- Judge a tension in clay lighting: mirror the inner shadows for diffuse, neutral light; bias them deliberately when the scene has a directional key. **Fails when:** inconsistent settings make objects appear to inhabit different lighting environments. ([clay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=76s))
- Build metal as a layered system: rounded base, multi-stop linear gradient, Overlay radial highlight, two inner shadows at different scales, and an optional tint. **Fails when:** the subject should read as matte plastic, paper, fabric, or another nonmetal material. ([metal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=112s))
- Create the metallic read with a controlled high-contrast value change near the gradient’s middle, using desaturated greys and cool dark blues. **Fails when:** the transition becomes a hard seam or the intended material is neutral and nonreflective. ([metal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=112s))
- Add specular sheen on a separate white radial layer set to Overlay, preserving the base value structure. **Fails when:** the base is so close to black or white that Overlay has no useful range. ([metal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=112s))
- Carve a machined edge with a tight inner shadow plus a broader one. **Fails when:** the final render is too small to keep the two scales distinct. ([metal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=112s))
- Recolour completed metal with a low-opacity solid tint—around 20%—rather than rebuilding the value gradient. Use warm red-orange families for gold, bronze, and rose finishes; use blue families for steel or titanium. **Fails when:** a stronger tint flattens the values into coloured plastic. ([metal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=112s))
- Reserve elaborate metallic treatment for subjects that plausibly are metal. **Fails when:** the effect becomes visually louder and more expensive than the object deserves. ([metal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSm6MA8bww&t=112s))

## Use badges and stickers as controlled disruption

- Add one sticker or badge when an otherwise competent composition feels trapped inside aligned rectangles; overlap a boundary to create a deliberate escape from the grid. **Fails when:** the surface is dense functional UI or stickers appear so often that disruption becomes the baseline. ([stickers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=341s))
- Generate regular burst or seal silhouettes from a high-point-count star or polygon, then tune inner radius and corner rounding parametrically. **Fails when:** the desired character is asymmetric or visibly hand-drawn. ([stickers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=341s))
- Set short badge copy in a quirky display face distinct from interface typography so the badge reads as an applied object rather than a UI chip. **Fails when:** copy is long, critical, small, or constrained by a strict single-typeface identity. ([stickers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=341s))
- Use a near-zero-blur, full-opacity offset shadow in a darker version of the sticker’s fill to imply a printed die-cut object. **Fails when:** the surrounding material system uses soft, physically modeled elevation. ([stickers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=341s))
- Add at most one or two secondary treatments—an offset outline or short curved microcopy—to make a large badge feel finished. **Fails when:** the badge is too small to preserve those details. ([stickers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=341s))
- Let decorative stickers vary in silhouette, saturation, and internal pattern because their role is maximalist interruption. **Fails when:** badges encode comparable status or pricing information and therefore require a consistent visual grammar. ([stickers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=341s))
- Reserve high-chroma kaleidoscopic badges for dark fields where their edges remain distinct. **Fails when:** the background is light or text must remain readable across the pattern. ([stickers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=341s))

## Art-direct packaging and physical products

- Research real category packaging to learn conventions and constraints, not to acquire a style to copy. **Fails when:** ignoring shelf conventions makes a retail product impossible to identify or evaluate. ([packaging](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=78s))
- For premium packaging, reduce the front face to the brand mark, product name, one descriptor, and required volume information. **Fails when:** value-led or mass-market positioning depends on visible benefits, claims, or regulatory information. ([packaging](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=78s))
- Establish one dominant alignment axis on small surfaces. When stacked elements differ sharply in width or weight, hold one on center and move the other intentionally toward an edge. **Fails when:** a fully symmetrical composition with matching widths is the stronger expression. ([packaging](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=78s))
- Use a stock or purchased mockup that accurately matches the product form when photography or rendering is not the team’s strength; spend the saved effort on the applied artwork. **Fails when:** the form is unusual or the mockup’s lighting conflicts with the visual system. ([packaging](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=78s))
- Apply the same custom-mockup discipline to packaging and e-commerce product shots because the image directly affects perceived value. **Fails when:** generated or templated imagery misrepresents the actual product. ([client presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=249s))

## Create and integrate 3D assets

- Start brand-accurate 3D logos, icons, and badges from the existing SVG, then extrude the vector rather than remodeling its silhouette. **Fails when:** the form is organic, needs genuine volume, or would look like a cookie cutter when simply extruded. ([3D build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=130s))
- Follow the dependency chain: import vector, extrude, frame with the camera, light, apply materials, preview motion, then export. **Fails when:** none; later judgments depend on earlier stages. ([3D build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=130s))
- Make convincing materials and reflections the quality differentiator for simple branded geometry. **Fails when:** the asset’s silhouette—not its surface—is the unresolved problem. ([3D build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=130s))
- Judge a tooling tension explicitly: choose Spline for quick iteration and immediate web-animation preview; choose Blender when material and render quality justify a steeper learning curve. **Fails when:** the output must ship as an interactive scene that the chosen pipeline cannot support. ([3D build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=130s))
- Budget several learning hours before expecting a usable result from an unfamiliar high-end 3D tool, and roughly two hours per simple hero asset while new to the process. **Fails when:** the asset requires rigging, simulation, characters, complex topology, or photoreal material development. ([3D build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=130s)) ([3D QA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=205s))
- Reproduce a tutorial material when speed matters, but accept it only through visual inspection. **Fails when:** the material must be varied, relit, debugged, or reused and nobody understands the node graph. ([3D build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=130s))
- Prototype a small asset in two viable tools and compare finished renders side by side when the tooling decision remains uncertain. **Fails when:** duplicating a large or complex build would waste the schedule. ([3D build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=130s))
- Preview animation inside the authoring tool and require it to play correctly before integration. Inspect an entire loop rather than one attractive pose. **Fails when:** none; motion defects often hide between key poses. ([3D build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=130s)) ([3D QA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=205s))
- Correct dark zones on a rotating opaque object with fill or rim lights placed around it, not by overdriving the existing key. **Fails when:** reflective or transparent materials depend primarily on the environment map rather than discrete lights. ([3D QA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=205s))
- Give a hero-scale 3D object sufficient visual space and, where appropriate, its own presentation moment. **Fails when:** repeated full-height sections push important content below screens of decoration. ([3D QA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=205s))
- In time-boxed exploration, ship a documented minor flaw as an explicit revision item. **Fails when:** the work is a final client delivery or brand-critical launch where visible defects undermine trust. ([3D QA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=205s))
- Offer lightweight 3D as a differentiated service only where performance, accessibility, and content priorities permit it. **Fails when:** the audience or platform cannot afford the asset’s weight and motion. ([3D QA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_Ozpb0XDuw&t=205s))

## Prepare product screenshots and mockups

- Use real, lightly retouched product screens as feature artwork instead of generic icons whenever the interface can demonstrate the feature. **Fails when:** the UI is unfinished, the feature is nonvisual, or the screenshot communicates nothing useful. ([product screens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PDcQJOPby1k&t=358s))
- Reuse existing interface work as marketing imagery to preserve continuity between promise and product. **Fails when:** the marketing claim describes functionality not represented by those screens. ([product screens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PDcQJOPby1k&t=358s))
- Retouch screenshots by removing irrelevant chrome, placeholder data, and unrelated regions while preserving an honest view of the shipped product. **Fails when:** the editing materially changes what users will receive. ([product screens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PDcQJOPby1k&t=358s))
- For dense interfaces, show one legible, tightly cropped feature at high zoom and add restrained motion rather than shrinking the entire dashboard. **Fails when:** the overall information architecture is the achievement being presented. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))
- Match showcase scale to information density: collages and perspective suit coarse landing-page screens; detailed dashboards usually require isolated crops. **Fails when:** distortion or reduction prevents viewers from reading what matters. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))
- Place a client-facing screen in a relevant device frame when platform, scale, and context of use need to feel concrete. **Fails when:** the meeting is a detailed critique of spacing, typography, or colour and the frame only shrinks the work. ([client presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=249s))
- Use current flagship hardware in aspirational mockups. Use the real older device when the product genuinely targets legacy hardware or a fixed enterprise fleet. **Fails when:** fashionable hardware would misrepresent the deployment context. ([client presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=249s))
- Escalate to a lifestyle scene—hands, posture, and real environment—when context of use matters more than screen detail. **Fails when:** the setting, lighting, or hands win attention over the interface. ([client presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=249s))
- Generate custom mockup photography when templates cannot supply the scene, but keep the interface itself hand-designed. **Fails when:** the model is asked to invent product hierarchy or interaction logic. ([client presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=249s))
- Write mockup prompts as a controlled specification: device, clean screen fill, supporting material, one or two props, depth of field, shadow character, lighting mood, and restrained imperfections. **Fails when:** excessive detail damages device geometry or makes the screen impossible to replace cleanly. ([client presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=249s))
- Add believable environmental irregularities such as wear, stains, or lived-in texture, but keep damage off the product being sold. **Fails when:** imperfection implies a defective commercial item. ([client presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=249s))
- Request a uniform keyed screen colour and perspective-warp the real interface into its four corners. Watch for coloured spill and conflicts with the screenshot palette. **Fails when:** the key contaminates reflective surroundings or removes intended screen colours. ([client presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=249s))

## Present the work as a branded composition

- Choose presentation treatment by audience. Use expressive, attention-winning staging for portfolios; use realistic, restrained staging when clients must judge the actual product. **Fails when:** the client is buying the expressive campaign or identity itself. ([presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=0s)) ([client presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=249s))
- Treat presentation as an independent layer that benefits quiet enterprise work as well as expressive design. **Fails when:** staging overwhelms the deliberate restraint of the underlying product. ([presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=0s))
- Resolve a central tension deliberately: default to a plain branded background and faint shadow because it is reliable; escalate only when the audience, medium, or brief rewards invention. **Fails when:** minimal treatment disappears in a visually competitive portfolio feed or expressive staging would reduce client trust. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))
- Separate a light interface from a plain field with a low-opacity, generously blurred shadow. **Fails when:** the intended material should sit flush or the shadow conflicts with the scene’s lighting. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))
- On dark showcases, replace invisible shadows with two or three large, heavily blurred accent-colour glows behind the interface. **Fails when:** used on light compositions where the glow reads as haze or a rendering defect. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))
- Define translucent presentation panels with a subtle gradient edge rather than a uniform line so the material catches light unevenly. **Fails when:** the composition does not otherwise support a glass-like material. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))
- Create an “exploded” hero by allowing one chart, pattern, slider, or brand-derived metric shape to cross the frame boundary. **Fails when:** viewers need an accurate representation of the real product layout. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))
- Use restrained perspective—only a few degrees on one axis and roughly ten to fifteen on the other—so motion does not destroy legibility. **Fails when:** the screen is text-dense or must be read closely. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))
- Emphasize one region in a skewed screen by lifting it from its original position and giving it an independent shadow; retain a muted placeholder only when it clarifies the displacement. **Fails when:** the ghost slot adds more clutter than explanation. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))
- Present multiple screens with hierarchy: offset them, let secondary frames bleed beyond the canvas, and use controlled stagger, rotation, or group skew to create movement. **Fails when:** documentation or specifications require a regular grid for scanning and comparison. ([showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=18s))
- Optimize marketing presentation craft—imagery, finishing, and credibility—before multiplying sections or decorative motion. **Fails when:** polish is compensating for an absent or unclear value proposition. ([product screens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PDcQJOPby1k&t=358s))

## Show behavior through time

- Build a clickable prototype whenever behavior is easier to demonstrate than describe. **Fails when:** the prototype adds no information beyond the static state. ([client presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=249s))
- Prototype affordances invisible at rest, including swipe actions, long-press menus, and gesture-revealed controls. **Fails when:** the delivery medium cannot reproduce the interaction and a short recording would communicate it more reliably. ([client presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=249s))
- Record the entry and exit choreography of overlays, creation flows, and destructive actions to demonstrate transition craft and edge-state completeness. **Fails when:** decorative motion slows or obscures a high-frequency task. ([client presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cTdCu8HMgM&t=249s))
- Keep expandable presentation content within a fixed visual footprint when possible; for pointer contexts, a detail panel may slide over expendable imagery. **Fails when:** touch users lack an explicit open-and-close control or the hidden material is decision-critical. ([photo extension](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=322s))
- Preserve the established text-to-visual rhythm across a showcase; collapse unusually long secondary detail behind an explicit disclosure control. **Fails when:** the long content is the primary reason viewers opened that section. ([photo extension](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=322s))

## Final review

- Review the composition at delivery scale and ask, in order: What is seen first? Does imagery identify the domain? Does every decorative layer support mood or meaning? Is the product represented honestly?
- Check that decoration never enters text safety zones, material effects remain plausible, crop choices preserve required information, and motion survives its full cycle.
- Record unresolved flaws and context-specific exceptions explicitly instead of silently normalizing them.
- Hand functional palette tokens, accessibility contrast, responsive structure, and interaction layout to the appropriate UI-system workflow; this skill governs branded expression and presentation, not interface engineering.
