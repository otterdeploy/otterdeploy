## Compose from goals and content

- Name the page’s single primary outcome before placing anything. Tie each section to that outcome; do not force a conversion frame onto documentation, reference sites, or internal tools. **Repeatedly supported.** ([goal-first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=261s), [focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6CC8lLnqa28&t=178s))
- Decide whether the artifact is a production interface or a showcase concept. Optimize production work for task completion; allow richer visual density in concept work, and explicitly disclose any usability trade-off. ([context](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=137s), [trade-offs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=192s), [concepts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=306s))
- List the information the user needs to make the page’s decision before choosing a layout. Remove fields that do not support that decision or move them to a detail view. ([content-first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=162s))
- Order sections by the questions a first-time visitor asks, not the organization chart or source-document order. Let each section answer one question and prepare the next. Override the assumed sequence when research or analytics shows different behavior. **Repeatedly supported.** ([page-order](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=261s), [section-flow](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=136s))
- Make the product category, value proposition, and next action understandable during a rapid scroll. Do not fragment one message across unrelated badges, pop-ups, shapes, and text scraps. ([rapid-read](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tNMAFjzapOk&t=22s))
- Apply the same goal-first reasoning regardless of client budget. Treat strategic depth and production budget as separate constraints. ([small-projects](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=261s))
- Present portfolio sections as goal, decision, reasoning, and outcome—not merely a screenshot gallery—unless execution craft itself is the product being hired. ([portfolio-story](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=261s))
- Treat launch as a starting point when planning conversion-oriented work; keep optimization, content, marketing, and maintenance opportunities separable rather than inflating the initial layout scope. ([ongoing-work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=261s))

## Work structure-first

- Gather references from shipped pages that solve the same section-level problem. Extract individual mechanics, rank multiple examples, and rebuild them around the current content instead of cloning a composition. **Repeatedly supported.** ([references](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=137s), [live-patterns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=72s), [section-research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=136s))
- Stop reference and mood-board work when further edits would not affect the eventual system or deliverable. Finish decisions such as type metrics and spacing scales when the layout will inherit them. ([stop-research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=137s))
- Wireframe each non-trivial section as plain boxes before styling. Include interactive controls in the wireframe and require the reading order to work without color, imagery, copy polish, or effects. **Repeatedly supported.** ([wireframe-cards](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=15s), [wireframe-gallery](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=228s), [wireframe-test](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tNMAFjzapOk&t=22s))
- Fix structure before styling when a page feels cluttered. Establish the split, stack, or grid before adjusting type, color, imagery, or motion. ([structure-first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=137s))
- Style and implement outside-in: resolve the page container, then section distribution, then nested groups, and only then local gaps. Keep outer positioning and inner distribution as separate layout concerns. ([nav-structure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d4MF6pdAZNw&t=569s), [outside-in](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d4MF6pdAZNw&t=630s))
- Let parent layouts own sibling spacing through a declared gap rather than child margins. Use nested layout groups only when the subgroup genuinely needs a different axis or distribution rule. ([layout-gaps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d4MF6pdAZNw&t=144s))
- Mark provisional dimensions as provisional and revisit them after rendering. Snap the final values back to the project’s spacing system. ([provisional-values](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d4MF6pdAZNw&t=630s))
- Establish hierarchy, spacing, imagery, and static affordances before adding motion. Add advanced interaction only after the still layout communicates correctly. **Repeatedly supported.** ([static-first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pGYLZyBE32o&t=197s), [polish-order](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=289s))

## Control attention

- Give each section one dominant focal point and one deliberate reading path. Increase the focal element’s relative size, position, or contrast instead of raising every element together. **Repeatedly supported.** ([hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=45s), [scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=175s), [attention-path](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tNMAFjzapOk&t=0s))
- Limit a conversion-focused hero or CTA section to one primary action. Keep a second action only for a distinct intent and render it with less weight; allow parallel actions when browsing or comparison is the task. **Repeatedly supported.** ([single-action](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6CC8lLnqa28&t=178s), [hero-actions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=306s))
- Match repeated CTA labels when they lead to the same destination. Different wording implies different outcomes. ([consistent-cta](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=136s))
- Choose the CTA form from the next real action. Use an input or location search when conversion begins with user-provided text or geography; use a button when the next step is a single destination. Preserve entered data across authentication. ([contextual-cta](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=382s), [input-cta](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pGYLZyBE32o&t=57s))
- Place an offer-specific action inside the option card it affects. Keep niche offerings secondary unless they are a primary revenue or audience goal. ([option-actions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pGYLZyBE32o&t=197s))
- Audit every attention-grabbing and interactive element. Remove anything without a unique job; if it must remain, demote it without violating contrast or affordance requirements. **Repeatedly supported.** ([attention-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6CC8lLnqa28&t=178s), [redundancy-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=296s), [minimum-elements](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1TvOcKdBVE&t=326s))
- Rebalance the survivors after deleting a loud control. Emphasis is relative, so removal can unintentionally promote what remains. ([rebalance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=296s))
- Permit a technically redundant element only for a named reason such as discoverability, consistency, or brand character. Do not use “judgment call” as permission to retain everything. ([exceptions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=296s))
- Treat the viewport as a budget that may remain partly empty. Do not add plausible-but-unimplemented controls merely to fill a compositional gap in production work. ([viewport-budget](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6CC8lLnqa28&t=178s), [balance-elements](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=249s))
- Use empty space as an active focal device. Ensure a large void reads as deliberate through strong alignment and enough content mass, not as a broken or unfinished section. ([intentional-space](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=649s))

## Build grids, spacing, and density

- Overlay a column grid before tuning individual margins. Use it rigorously for repeating units and as a scaffold—not a prison—for one-off heroes and expressive sections. **Repeatedly supported.** ([alignment-grid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=115s), [grid-judgment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=124s))
- Define responsive spans against a predictable column contract such as 12 desktop, 8 tablet, and 4 mobile columns. Change the contract when the content or implementation system requires another model. ([responsive-grid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=124s))
- Allow a small number of deliberate grid breaks, overlaps, offsets, or overhangs after the underlying alignment is sound. Remove them when they disrupt reading order, wrapping, or small-screen behavior. **Repeatedly supported.** ([grid-breaks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=115s), [controlled-overlap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=77s), [asymmetry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=441s))
- Choose a compact spacing scale, preferably 4px-based, and reuse it across screens. Treat the scale as a consistency mechanism rather than proof that every multiple is aesthetically correct. ([spacing-scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=124s))
- Start a stacked section with one baseline sibling gap, then tighten only the relationships that form a unit. Make between-group spacing roughly two to three times the within-group spacing when room permits. **Repeatedly supported.** ([section-gaps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=124s), [proximity](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=81s))
- Set type size and line height before finalizing section gaps, because line boxes contribute to perceived spacing. Remove unintended text trim where tight auto-layout components gain phantom space. ([type-and-space](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=124s), [text-trim](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=115s))
- Rebuild repeated cards, chips, and controls with declared padding and gaps so content changes do not disturb their internal alignment. Apply the same spacing treatment everywhere the pattern recurs. ([repeatable-spacing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=115s))
- Budget more vertical separation on narrow screens when stacking removes surrounding horizontal space. Then validate that the extra space has not turned the mobile page into needless scrolling. ([mobile-space](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=115s))
- Test density by scanning for one known item. If tighter spacing slows retrieval, treat that as a usability regression rather than a matter of taste. ([density-test](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1TvOcKdBVE&t=326s))
- Count the full cost of cards: padding, borders, and gutters multiply across every repeated item. Prefer open groups when container chrome consumes more space than it clarifies. ([card-overhead](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=81s))
- When removing boxes, transfer their separating function into larger gaps and consistent sibling styling. Do not simply delete the border and collapse the content together. ([open-groups](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=81s))
- Use a width-filling constrained container for normal page content and let the exterior absorb ultrawide space. Consider continuous vertical rules only when the ruled frame is part of the page’s structural language. ([bounded-width](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=289s))
- Evaluate candidate layouts by whether they accept another section and collapse cleanly at narrow widths. Reject desktop compositions that require bespoke reconstruction at every breakpoint. ([layout-resilience](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=87s))

## Separate with the lightest sufficient device

- Use shared column edges, aligned rows and semantic sections to replace redundant nested cards. **Fails when:** Retain a container when it communicates an independent group or interaction. ([scannable-ui](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=neE6wOuBIP8&t=426s))
- Preserve grouping, recognizable controls and restrained default states when removing card wrappers. **Fails when:** A flat undifferentiated list loses the structure the cards supplied. ([scannable-ui](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=neE6wOuBIP8&t=426s))
- Rank separation methods from lightest to heaviest: whitespace, subtle surface change, then a drawn line. Escalate only after the lighter method fails. **Repeatedly supported.** ([whitespace-first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=232s), [separation-order](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1TvOcKdBVE&t=326s))
- Remove borders and strokes that do not separate, indicate state, define a hit target, or meet an accessibility need. ([justify-strokes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=296s))
- Use a restrained alternating row fill when a dense table cannot afford adequate whitespace. Keep the contrast low and distinct from selection, hover, and error states. ([dense-rows](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1TvOcKdBVE&t=326s))
- When rules are a deliberate motif, define one weight, low-contrast color, inset, and geometry system and repeat it selectively. Curves and cornered lines may reinforce the page’s shape language. ([divider-system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=232s))
- Match divider weight and frequency to the intended tone and subject. Hairlines with space can feel technical; heavier repeated lines can feel editorial or print-like. Reject a fashionable treatment that feels borrowed from an unrelated category. ([divider-tone](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=232s))
- Remove rules when they box content into cramped cells. A ruled grid fails when padding is insufficient or items need unequal emphasis. ([divider-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHwZzWd-OnQ&t=232s))
- Use a continuous ruled grid for three to eight short, equally weighted items when shared structure matters more than individual card affordance. Use cards or varied tiles when items differ sharply or must feel independently interactive. ([ruled-grid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=342s))
- Repeat a structural motif in at least two separated locations, but not in every section. Keep grid lines static if motion occurs inside their cells. ([motif-reuse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=342s))

## Compose heroes and navigation

- Default to familiar top navigation and normal reading order. Place the brand at the top-left and the primary action where it is easy to find; deviate only for a specific user benefit or a different reading direction. **Repeatedly supported.** ([conventions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=87s), [hero-nav](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=143s))
- Give the header a clear two-tier hierarchy: plain navigation for secondary destinations and one stronger conversion action. Collapse low-frequency utilities only when hiding them does not conceal important state or conversion paths. ([header-hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=136s), [header-space](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=192s))
- Use a typographic wordmark in the prime brand position when no standalone logo exists. ([wordmark](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=192s))
- Strip utility actions to their minimum understandable form; do not render every header item as a button. Keep labels where icon meaning is not universal. ([nav-weight](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=249s))
- Use dropdowns or mega menus only for destinations with substantial depth. Add meaningful thumbnails when visual recognition helps, but omit generic imagery and avoid menus that exceed the viewport. ([expanded-nav](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=342s), [menu-depth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=289s))
- Build a conversion hero from a short headline, brief supporting line, and one dominant CTA; add one quieter secondary route only when required. ([hero-stack](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=306s), [brief-copy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=136s))
- Use a centered SaaS hero as a low-risk starting point when copy stays within two or three lines. Switch to left alignment when the text becomes longer or the visual needs a directional reading path. ([saas-hero](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=87s))
- Use a wide image/copy split when category convention and strong imagery support it. Restack headline, subhead, CTA, and visual when either column becomes cramped or on narrow screens. **Tension—judge from content and viewport.** ([split-hero](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=137s), [stacked-hero](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=136s))
- Anchor a wide split to one side and group supporting imagery on the other. Avoid evenly distributing several competing centers of gravity. ([hero-anchor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=192s))
- Remove social links, testimonials, site outlines, and secondary modules from the hero unless they directly resolve the audience’s main objection. Relocate useful proof below rather than discarding it. ([hero-cleanup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=137s))
- Use actual product UI or representative work as the main visual proof for software and portfolios. Crop to the exact region that supports the claim; show the full interface only when breadth itself is the value. **Repeatedly supported.** ([product-proof](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=136s), [screenshot-crop](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=441s), [portfolio-proof](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=367s))
- Pair extremely large headline type with equally substantial media. Reserve the 15:1-scale, headline-first editorial pattern for very short statements and brand-led pages; do not use it on utility-heavy sites or long copy. ([giant-hero](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=144s))
- Keep giant display type condensed, simple, and legible at the intended scale. Do not reuse it for body text or allow it to overflow the real viewport. ([display-type](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=144s))
- For a multi-work portfolio hero, rotate strong images only when breadth is the message. Keep one static conversion visual when rotation would compete with a single product proposition. ([hero-slideshow](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=144s))
- Use product-native motion as ambient hero decoration when it demonstrates behavior. Keep the primary action immediately usable and honor reduced-motion preferences. ([native-motion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pGYLZyBE32o&t=57s))

## Fit heroes to real imagery

- Source media at or above its rendered dimensions and inspect it at 100% size. Use placeholders only to unblock structure, then replace them before judging finish; preserve the expected aspect ratio and focal zone. **Repeatedly supported.** ([placeholder](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=137s), [hero-resolution](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=143s), [full-bleed-media](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=144s))
- Select images by intended frame ratio, subject scale, focal position, lighting, and available text space. Reject assets that require destructive cropping or place the subject beneath fixed overlays. **Repeatedly supported.** ([image-filter](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=249s), [gallery-ratio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=228s), [overlay-zone](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=203s))
- Prefer wider, more distant hero shots when text or an information bar needs negative space. Use tight crops only when confrontation or close detail is the intended statement. ([hero-crop](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=143s))
- Keep text away from the image’s focal subject. Reposition it into negative space before attempting masks or compositing. ([subject-clearance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=143s))
- Use silhouette masking or knocked-out type only when the subject edge is crisp and the remaining letterforms stay readable. Restrict cutouts to heavy, simple display type and a minimal image-plus-type structure. ([subject-mask](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=143s), [cutout-type](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=566s))
- Do not force primary CTAs into a cutout treatment when it weakens button contrast or affordance. ([cutout-controls](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=566s))
- Put text over photography only after testing contrast as a separate step. Prefer moving the text or changing the image; otherwise add a localized gradient or subtle scrim that preserves the image. Omit the scrim when the text region already has stable contrast. **Tension—use the least intervention that remains robust.** ([image-contrast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=249s), [gradient-scrim](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=72s), [scrim-exception](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=382s))
- Put overlaid icons, labels, and controls on a solid or translucent surface so contrast does not depend on the photo. Prefer a separate control strip when space allows. ([image-controls](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=162s), [control-surface](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=601s))
- Validate every image-backed treatment against light, dark, and visually busy samples. ([image-stress-test](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=601s))

## Shape page rhythm

- Vary the structures of adjacent narrative sections—full bleed, open text, cards, bento, ruled grid, or columns—rather than repeating the same image/text split. Keep systematic comparison layouts structurally consistent. **Repeatedly supported.** ([section-variety](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=26s), [avoid-repetition](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=262s))
- Reuse established spacing, card treatments, and structural motifs even while varying section composition. Consistency and variety operate at different levels. ([reuse-language](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=306s), [card-system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=15s))
- When repeating an image/text block, reverse the middle block or alter one controlled variable so the scroll zigzags. Do not accumulate unrelated typefaces or sacrifice small-screen reading order. ([alternation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=382s))
- Re-audit rhythm whenever a section is inserted, removed, or reordered; variety is a property of the complete sequence. ([sequence-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=382s))
- Design section seams deliberately with shared subjects, overlaps, fades, or aligned structural lines. Keep overlaps modest so they do not obscure hierarchy or break responsive behavior. ([section-seams](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=289s), [small-overlaps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=77s))
- Use a short full-bleed image band as an interlude between dense text sections. Limit it to one meaningful line, ensure the image survives an extreme-wide crop, and omit the band when it adds only filler or extra scroll. ([image-interlude](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=72s))
- Insert a text-only section after several image-led sections when the copy deserves full attention. Give it enough vertical room and a paragraph with sufficient visual mass; do not use it for thin filler or inherently visual content. ([text-only](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=649s))
- Keep section height proportional to content. Tighten padding after reducing copy; allow near-viewport sections when one idea per scroll drives the narrative, but avoid forced scrolling on comparison pages. **Tension—optimize either immersion or scan speed.** ([compact-sections](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=289s), [viewport-sections](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=151s))
- Remove sections that repeat the same image, statistics, and message. Retain purposeful repetition only near a later conversion point where reinforcement helps. ([remove-repeats](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=262s))
- After completing a section, ask whether one additional element would materially improve balance. Add at most one, and only when it does not introduce task friction. ([one-more-pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=306s))
- End the page’s value-and-trust narrative with a direct next step unless pricing, eligibility, or setup information is still required. ([closing-cta](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pGYLZyBE32o&t=300s))

## Compose cards in context

- Choose cards only when individual containment, interaction, or mixed-height separation is useful. Use whitespace or a shared ruled structure when detached boxes add only chrome. ([cards-in-context](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=81s))
- Rank card content first through size, vertical position, alignment, and contrast. Add borders, shadows, or dividers only if those levers cannot communicate the structure. ([card-hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=45s))
- Put the primary identifier first and make it visibly stronger. Demote contextual metadata unless time or status is the decision-driving value. ([card-order](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=45s))
- Give decision-driving values such as price or status a separate alignment zone and restrained accent. Do not accent several competing values. ([decision-value](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=45s))
- Use meaningful leading imagery when it accelerates recognition. Omit generic placeholders that contribute no information. ([card-imagery](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=45s))
- Keep peer-card imagery consistent: use one crop and surface treatment across the row. Vary treatments only when an asymmetric layout already establishes that the tiles are not peers. ([image-consistency](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=203s))
- Keep titles outside replaceable card imagery so contrast remains stable. Reserve overlaid titles for art-directed assets that will not change. ([card-titles](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=203s))
- Put only comparison and shortlisting fields on browse cards. Reduce prose to one useful line or move it to the detail view. ([listing-cards](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=162s))
- Give dynamic text an explicit overflow rule and test longest, shortest, missing, and messy values. Preserve full strings when clipping would hide decision-critical or legal information. ([dynamic-content](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=162s))
- Place paired values on shared alignment with understandable visual connectors; retain accessible text when icon meaning is ambiguous. ([paired-values](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY&t=45s))
- Decide inline-action placement against the card’s actual image and reading path. Keep primary actions visible; reveal only secondary actions on hover or focus, with an equivalent touch path. ([card-action](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=249s), [secondary-actions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=441s))
- Use a small set label or chip only when it adds meaningful categorization. Repeated decorative chips dilute the signal. ([section-chip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=249s))
- Keep dense card gutters and outer section padding mathematically consistent; make outer padding at least as large as internal gutters so the set reads as inset. ([contained-cards](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=15s))

## Choose bento grids deliberately

- Use a bento grid only when enough substantive content exists to fill every cell and items need unequal space or emphasis. Choose a simpler stack or regular grid when cells would remain sparse. ([bento-fit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=484s))
- Treat cell count and proportions as provisional until real copy and imagery arrive. Give one discrete idea to each cell and recut the grid instead of cramming content into the wireframe. ([bento-content](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=484s))
- Match the grid to the aspect ratios of available assets. Let portrait material drive tall cells instead of forcing destructive wide crops. ([bento-assets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=262s))
- Vary tile sizes by content weight while retaining an underlying orthogonal grid. Break shared edges only after the base alignment reads clearly. ([bento-hierarchy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=289s))
- Adapt references by changing a structural variable such as container width, but cap full-bleed bento grids on ultrawide displays when cells become unreadably stretched. ([bento-adaptation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=484s))

## Choose carousels and galleries deliberately

- Use a horizontal track when three to ten peer items must fit into a shallow section and simultaneous comparison is not essential. Use a grid, filterable list, or pagination for large, open-ended, printable, or comparison-heavy sets. ([carousel-fit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=392s))
- Preserve card proportions by overflowing a track rather than shrinking cards until they become illegible. Keep critical items visible because off-screen content receives less attention. ([card-slider](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=249s))
- Use one large fixed image as the anchor and a scrollable secondary set when a gallery needs both focus and breadth. Skip the interaction when all images fit comfortably. ([gallery-anchor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=228s))
- Pair gallery imagery with its explanatory copy inside one section so the visual is evidence rather than decoration. ([gallery-copy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=228s))
- Provide persistent position information through dots, a counter, or progress bar. Use a counter or bar rather than dozens of dots for long sets. **Repeatedly supported.** ([gallery-position](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=228s), [carousel-indicator](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=601s))
- On desktop, provide visible focusable controls and support direct dragging. Do not make tiny arrows the only navigation path. ([carousel-controls](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=392s))
- On touch, prioritize swipe and avoid redundant arrow clutter, but retain accessible focusable navigation and a visible overflow cue. **Tension—gesture minimalism must not erase discoverability or keyboard access.** ([touch-controls](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH_ugxmLeUM&t=296s), [desktop-controls](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=228s))
- Group arrows and indicators into one stable control cluster when practical. Prefer a strip below the media; use a translucent blurred surface only when the image must remain edge-to-edge. ([control-cluster](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=601s))
- Show auto-advance timing on the active indicator and provide pause behavior. Do not imply a timer on manually advanced carousels. ([carousel-timer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=601s))
- Keep carousel sections restrained to heading, track, and controls. Use eased continuous transitions; reserve focal-item scaling for expressive sites where motion will not impair scanning or performance. ([carousel-motion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE&t=392s), [eased-motion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=289s))
- Validate the consolidated controls at mobile width without relying on a layout-specific rearrangement. ([responsive-carousel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=601s))

## Place proof and product information

- Put credible testimonials, named outcomes, or client logos on the homepage when trust is the primary objection. Omit thin or invented proof. ([services-proof](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=261s))
- For nonprofits, lead with recent, concrete impact figures instead of a generic mission statement. Use vision and plan when no track record exists yet. ([impact-proof](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5JxUJ1fuyO8&t=261s))
- Place a real customer-logo strip immediately after a B2B or SaaS hero. Select the most recognizable or audience-authoritative names, not the largest possible count. ([logo-proof](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=0s))
- Label the logo relationship when it could mean customers, integrations, investors, or press. Otherwise keep the band free of explanatory paragraphs. ([logo-meaning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=0s))
- Use a static evenly spaced logo row when the set fits. Use a marquee only for justified overflow or polish, respect motion sensitivity, and keep each mark readable. ([logo-layout](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=0s))
- Fade an overflowing logo or card rail into a flat page background rather than hard-clipping it. Use progressive blur sparingly because stacked blur layers can harm animation performance and fail over complex backgrounds. ([rail-fade](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=87s))
- Repeat proof later only at a distinct decision point on a long page. Avoid repetition within the same short-page viewing region. ([repeat-proof](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=0s))
- Put comparative claims in a real customer’s attributed voice when direct brand claims would seem self-serving. Avoid naming competitors when doing so merely advertises stronger alternatives. ([comparison-proof](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=87s))
- Pull three or four decision-driving specifications directly beneath a product hero. Keep long, qualified specification sets in a dedicated detail area. ([spec-bar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=203s))
- Include explicit privacy and security reassurance when the product handles user data; choose symbols that fit the product language without sacrificing recognition. ([trust-section](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pGYLZyBE32o&t=300s))

## Handle optional and interactive detail

- Consolidate substantially overlapping offers into one comparison section built around the few factors users need. Keep separate pages when audiences, decisions, or conversion paths differ materially. ([offer-consolidation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pGYLZyBE32o&t=197s))
- Place optional, SKU-specific, or minority-audience details behind triggers attached to the products they concern. Promote them to a full section when they are a core differentiator or revenue driver. ([optional-detail](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=382s))
- Provide one configuration-specific trigger per eligible option when eligibility itself is useful decision information. ([configuration-triggers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=382s))
- Give every overlay a visible close control and keep supplementary modals to one idea, one image, and concise copy. Allow greater complexity only when the overlay is itself a real task surface. ([modal-scope](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=382s))
- Prefer visible image-forward features to accordions on consumer marketing pages. Use accordions for dense reference material where selective expansion reduces search cost. ([feature-disclosure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=262s))
- Use interactive cards, tabs, selectors, or sliders to compact optional explanation—not to hide information required for immediate comparison, touch access, or accessibility. ([interactive-detail](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pGYLZyBE32o&t=197s))
- Keep a persistent panel open while swapping sibling contents so users retain context. Close it fully when moving between unrelated destinations. ([persistent-panels](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=441s))

## Compose the footer from its inventory

- Choose the footer structure from actual link count. Use a centered stack for roughly three to five links and grouped columns for a genuinely deep inventory. ([footer-count](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=470s))
- Order a small footer as brand, navigation, then social links. Put copyright and credits in a lower utility bar so they remain available without competing. ([footer-order](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=470s))
- Preserve a familiar grouped-column footer when many legal, regional, and product links are mandatory, but reorganize it when labels, grouping, or accessibility are poor. ([dense-footer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3Omp1hm0Sg&t=382s))
- Give the footer a deliberate final design pass and verify every supported theme and responsive width. ([footer-validation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKM6b2EnW1k&t=470s))

## Validate in real viewports

- Define the true initial viewport before refining the hero. Subtract browser and system chrome, draw the usable-area boundary over the canvas, and treat content below it as scroll content. **Repeatedly supported.** ([desktop-viewport](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=192s), [safe-area](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=386s))
- Use roughly 1920×1000 only as a starting approximation for a maximized 1080p desktop browser. Measure laptops, ultrawides, kiosks, and dynamic mobile browser chrome rather than reusing that number blindly. ([viewport-size](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=192s))
- When a desktop composition exceeds the intended first view, compress non-semantic vertical slack before shrinking legible type or cropping required content. ([fit-the-fold](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=192s))
- Distrust spacious portfolio screenshots as production measurements. Revalidate their proportions against the real viewport, while allowing expected scroll on long-form marketing pages. ([showcase-height](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvbFPzLjWcU&t=192s))
- View designs at 100% on target-class hardware before finalizing type, spacing, crop, reach, and tap behavior. Mirror mobile prototypes to a physical phone, then test additional viewport classes rather than trusting one device. ([real-device](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp6ey4AyDzA&t=386s))
- Test responsive behavior with real long, short, missing, and messy content—not only tidy placeholders. ([real-data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE4rLEQpiXY&t=162s))
- Verify that overflow, overlap, staggered offsets, full-bleed elements, and edge-breaking treatments do not clip content or create horizontal scrolling on narrow screens. ([responsive-overflow](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=77s), [edge-break](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pGYLZyBE32o&t=300s))
- Audit the near-final page layer by layer: objective, reading path, section order, viewport fit, grid, spacing, imagery, copy, proof, controls, responsive behavior, accessibility, and motion. Improve each layer one notch instead of masking structural problems with one signature effect. ([premium-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=441s))
- Toggle subtle gradients, blur, shadows, and overlays off to verify that each one materially improves depth or legibility. Remove effects that become obvious, reduce contrast, or add no value. ([subtle-polish](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPeTgU7la34&t=151s))
- Validate motion for keyboard, touch, performance, and reduced-motion behavior. Use stagger, blur, hover, and entrance effects only after they preserve immediate access to content. ([motion-validation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulSOdTgoGeY&t=367s), [motion-craft](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMMiLeo_UGI&t=441s))
- Finish only when a first-time user can identify the page’s subject, focal message, and intended next action within seconds and every section earns its place in the scroll. ([final-audit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6CC8lLnqa28&t=178s))
